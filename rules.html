How verification works

One rule decides everything below: we judge the making, not the subject. A channel about AI can carry the mark. A channel that generated its video cannot.

What the mark means

A mark says one thing: this video, or this channel, was made by people. It is not a statement about quality, and it is not an endorsement. The list is positive, so a video without a mark has simply not been checked. Silence is not an accusation.

What earns a mark

A video qualifies when the work in it is human work: roughly nine tenths or more of what you watch. The rest is judgement, and the judgement is written down here rather than left to mood.

  • Tools do not disqualify. Removing a background, cropping, colour grading, cleaning audio, transcribing your own speech, or using an editor whose features happen to be model powered. A tool may help; it may not author.
  • Generated work at the core does. A voice that replaced the author's own, footage passed off as real, a script written by a model instead of the author, music that is the point of the video, or a thumbnail pasted straight out of a prompt.
  • A slip is not a verdict. Twenty seconds of generated voice in a twenty minute stream does not erase the stream. Repeated output built on a generator does.

Video and channel are judged apart

A verified channel carries its videos, and a single video can be marked the other way on its own. One bad video does not remove a channel: the first one is a note on the file, not a deletion, and how much it weighs depends on how much the channel has made. A channel whose output is mostly pasted out of a prompt loses the mark.

How a decision is made

  1. A channel arrives by suggestion, or is found while reviewing something else.
  2. A person watches a sample of its videos, end to end where it matters.
  3. The verdict is recorded with the date and what it rested on, so it can be argued with later.

At this stage channels are added by hand, one at a time, and only channels whose work is well known to be human made. The rules are meant to survive contact with a larger list, which is why the open questions below stay open rather than being settled quietly.

How to argue with it

Open the panel on any video page. If a video carries a mark you disagree with, report it and say what you saw. If a channel you trust is missing, suggest it. Voting on contested marks is planned, not built: how long a channel keeps a contested mark will be decided in public, with the community, before it exists.

Still undecided

  • How to measure the share of human work when a video mixes sources.
  • Whether a one-off slip leaves the mark alone or adds a visible note on the video.
  • How many slips, against how many videos, cost a channel its mark.
  • Whether generated music under an otherwise human video counts against it.
